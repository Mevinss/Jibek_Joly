# TurkiSib

Web demo of an advisory railway ML/LLM service for a four-person hackathon team. Primary users are presenters, judges and dispatchers exploring a synthetic rail scenario.

The entry surface is the Kazakhstan dispatcher at `services/ai/web/index.html`, with `dispatch.css` and `dispatch.js`. Its map, train diagram, station scheme, forecast inspector and chat share train selection. Selecting a train also selects its next station. Geography and train routes are Kazakhstan-only; SIM identifiers and demonstration-input labels distinguish the synthetic scenario from operational ҚТЖ data. Station coordinates are sourced, but connecting geometry, distances, movements and track layouts are approximate or synthetic. No live train control is performed.

The dispatcher receives scenario snapshots through SSE, supports pause, speed, incidents and recorded-history replay, and exports the recorded history as CSV. “Прямой эфир” means the current demonstration stream. Locally bundled MapLibre supports the map; the SVG alternative remains available without WebGL. The optional OSM background needs internet. The previous model workbench remains at `services/ai/web/lab.html`, using `style.css` and `app.js`, for input experiments and model evidence.

The user chose a working screen immediately and delegated the stack. Serve plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript from the existing Python FastAPI service, without a Node build requirement. Interface language: Russian. Support desktop and mobile, keyboard use and reduced motion.

The trained LightGBM delay models predict next-segment delay change and a calibrated proxy risk. The legacy contract field `p_conflict_15m` is not a validated 15-minute conflict probability. Passenger models were trained on PKP data and their transfer to Kazakhstan is not locally validated; unsupported train types use `rule_fallback`, visibly labelled as a heuristic, not ML. SHAP contributions describe classifier log-odds before calibration, not causality or percentage-point effects.

The uploaded Kazakhstan ZIP is a synthetic schedule package. Its separate `kz-synthetic-schedule-v1` model learns scheduled duration for an entire segment, not actual delay or the impact of an incident. Its synthetic test MAE is not accuracy on real Kazakhstan journeys and must remain separate from PKP delay metrics. Both outputs come from actual models; improved predictions and optimizer benefits must never be fabricated. The diagram's dashed continuation extrapolates current speed and is not a solver plan. Solver, ATO and a validated quality index are outside this demo. API credentials and OpenAI calls remain server-side.

Primary success: the user can follow a selected SIM train across the map and diagrams, inspect measured predictions, pause to change delay or reserve, and obtain chat answers grounded in the snapshot captured at submission. Input experiments do not rewrite the timetable. Snapshot chat must use only that supplied state and its forecasts, without unrelated fixture plans or solver results. Connection loss, unavailable predictions and chat failures remain visible. A future landing page must preserve these factual boundaries.
